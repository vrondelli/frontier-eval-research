## Features (fixed definitions; use exactly these)

Extract exactly these six features from a candidate answer. Judge each feature only from that answer's own text together with the Problem it answers.

- `f_core` (0, 1, or 2): 2 means the answer states a complete construction or procedure that addresses every constraint stated in the Problem. 1 means the answer states a partial construction or procedure that addresses some but not all of the stated constraints, or leaves part of the construction unspecified. 0 means the answer states no construction or procedure at all.
- `f_check` (0 or 1): 1 means the answer reports a check actually applied to its own proposal, with an outcome stated as a specific value or observed result. A check that is only proposed for the future counts as `f_next`, not `f_check`. A generic assurance that the proposal works, with no applied check and no stated outcome, is `f_check`=0.
- `f_compare` (0 or 1): 1 means the answer compares at least two alternatives and gives a reason for choosing one over another. Merely naming two alternatives with no reason for the choice is `f_compare`=0.
- `f_contra` (0 or 1): 1 means the answer states or uses something that contradicts a constraint explicitly stated in the Problem (for example using a material, tool, or method the Problem excludes). Otherwise `f_contra`=0.
- `f_limit` (0 or 1): 1 means the answer states a limitation of its proposal or a condition under which the proposal fails. Merely leaving part of the work unfinished is not a limitation statement, so it is `f_limit`=0 by itself.
- `f_next` (0 or 1): 1 means the answer proposes a specific next step or future test, naming what would be done. A generic intention such as planning to think more about it, with no specific step named, is `f_next`=0.

The score is an ordinal from 0 to 3.

+# Assessment criteria
+
+## Rationale
+
+These criteria reward answers that give a complete, constraint-aware construction and that support it with an observed check. They also reward an explained choice between alternatives, since that makes the design decision inspectable. Explicitly contradicting a stated constraint receives the lowest score, and incomplete procedures score below complete ones. Limitations and named next steps are recorded as features so readers can identify them consistently, but they do not change the score unless a mapping row says so. The mapping is applied mechanically, without adjusting for style, length, or overall impression.
+
+## Feature definitions
+
+- `f_core` (0, 1, or 2): Assign 2 when the answer gives a complete construction or procedure addressing every constraint in the Problem. Assign 1 when it gives a partial construction or procedure addressing some constraints, or leaves some part unspecified. Assign 0 when it gives no construction or procedure.
+- `f_check` (0 or 1): Assign 1 only when the answer says it actually checked its own proposal and reports a specific value or observed result. A future check is not an applied check; a general assurance without a reported result is 0.
+- `f_compare` (0 or 1): Assign 1 when the answer weighs at least two alternatives and gives a reason for selecting one. Naming alternatives without a reason for the choice is 0.
+- `f_contra` (0 or 1): Assign 1 when the answer states or relies on something that conflicts with a constraint explicitly given in the Problem, such as an excluded material, tool, or method. Otherwise assign 0.
+- `f_limit` (0 or 1): Assign 1 when the answer identifies a limitation of its proposal or a condition in which it would fail. Incompleteness alone does not count; otherwise assign 0.
+- `f_next` (0 or 1): Assign 1 when the answer names a specific future action or test. A nonspecific intention to think further is 0.
+
+## Mapping table
+

+|---|---|---|

+| 6 | `f_core`=2 and `f_compare`=1 | 2 |
+| 7 | `f_core`=2 | 1 |
+| 8 | otherwise | 0 |
