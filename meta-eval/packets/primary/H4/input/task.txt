BUSINESS RULES - these are the requirement, exactly:

Rule K (pass code validity and class): A passcode string is valid if and only if it consists of exactly five ASCII digits and nothing else, or of exactly five ASCII digits, a single hyphen, and exactly three ASCII digits, and nothing else. Invalid therefore includes any string with surrounding whitespace, a space where the hyphen belongs, the longer form written without its hyphen, too few or too many digits in either part, letters or any other characters, a leading plus sign, or an empty string. The class of a record with a valid passcode string is determined by the number formed by the first two digits of the passcode string: the class is "youth" when that number is less than or equal to eighteen; it is "adult" when that number is greater than eighteen and less than or equal to forty-nine; it is "resident" when that number is greater than forty-nine and less than or equal to seventy-three; it is "priority" when that number is greater than seventy-three. Rule V (fare and voucher validity): A fare or baggage string is valid if and only if it consists of one or more ASCII digits, optionally followed by a decimal point and one or more ASCII digits, and nothing else, and the exact decimal value it denotes is greater than zero. Invalid therefore includes any string with surrounding whitespace, a leading plus or minus sign, thousands separators, underscores, exponent notation, a decimal point with no digit before it, a decimal point with no digit after it, an empty string, letters or any other characters, and any string denoting zero. Invalid voucher therefore includes any string with surrounding whitespace, a leading plus sign, thousands separators, underscores, exponent notation, a decimal point with no digit before it, a decimal point with no digit after it, an empty string, a minus sign alone, or letters or any other characters. A record with an invalid fare, baggage, or voucher string is excluded and contributes nothing else. Each excluded record appears exactly once in the excluded list, in input order, whatever combination of its strings is invalid.

Rule D (sailing date validity and eligibility): A sailed string is valid if and only if it consists of exactly four ASCII digits, a single hyphen, exactly two ASCII digits, a single hyphen, and exactly two ASCII digits, and nothing else, where the first group is the year, the second group is the month, and the third group is the day, the month number is greater than or equal to one and less than or equal to twelve, and the day number is greater than or equal to one and less than or equal to thirty-one. Invalid therefore includes any string with surrounding whitespace, slashes or any other separator in place of either hyphen, too few or too many digits in any group, a month number of zero or greater than twelve, a day number of zero or greater than thirty-one, letters or any other characters, or an empty string. Rule T (chargeable amount): For each record that is not excluded, the combined charge in whole currency units is its fare value plus its baggage value, computed on the exact decimal values denoted by the strings. The rebate in whole currency units is the smaller of nine hundredths of the combined charge and eighteen. The raw chargeable amount is the combined charge minus the rebate. The chargeable amount in whole currency units is the raw chargeable amount rounded upward to the nearest quarter of a unit: the smallest multiple of one quarter that is greater than or equal to the raw chargeable amount. A raw chargeable amount that is already an exact multiple of one quarter is unchanged by this rounding. No other rounding of any charge amount takes place at any point.

Rule W (settled amount): For each record that is not excluded, amounts are in whole currency units and are computed on exact decimal values, with no rounding until the final conversion to minor units described at the end of this rule. Every settled record also carries a handling charge of two. The subtotal of a record is its class levy plus its class rate multiplied by its chargeable amount plus the handling charge. The surcharged subtotal is the subtotal increased by seven hundredths of the subtotal, that is, the subtotal multiplied by one plus seven hundredths. A record in the priority class then adds a flat priority charge of six after the surcharge increase, and this priority charge is not itself increased by the surcharge percentage; records in other classes add nothing at that step. The amount of a record is its surcharged subtotal, plus the priority charge when it applies, minus its voucher value, so a negative voucher increases the amount.
