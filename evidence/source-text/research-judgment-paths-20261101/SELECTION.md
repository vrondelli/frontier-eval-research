## The most useful review findings

Two agents independently reviewed all three D1 artifacts before reading one another's critique. Review A (historical reference: `analysis/design-review-a.md`) and Review B (historical reference: `analysis/design-review-b.md`) preserve their separate judgments, proposal hashes, limitations and proposed repairs. Agent agreement is not expert gold.

**Rule versus application.** In proposal r1, E3's level2anchor requires a feasible route, a material limitation **and a next check**. Example B contains a route and limitations, but no candidate-proposed check. The proposal nevertheless assigns E3=2; a separate assessor field supplies the next check. Reviewer A identifies this as a narrow anchor/application inconsistency. A matched pair—B as written versus B plus an explicit proposed check—can test it. This does not establish the whole proposal is invalid or force a particular replacement score.

**Overlapping cooperation anchors.** Proposal r3 describes level0as no actionable response and level1as relevant intention without a workable process. Example D's generic cooperative intention can satisfy both descriptions. Both reviewers request clearer tie-breaking. This is an operational ambiguity, not evidence that an agent has measured or failed to measure sincerity.

**Reference scope.** Proposal r1 requires frozen-repertoire comparison but calls familiar examples A/F non-novel without instantiating that repertoire. Reviewer B asks for an explicit manifest or conditional classification. Familiarity can support a repertoire entry if that is actually the declared policy; no global false-novelty failure is established.

Other conditional risks concern ambiguous communication anchors, independent units/denominators, and ASR experiments: changing the answer's propositions through transcription error is different from changing style while preserving those propositions. Reviewers distinguish these risks from actual contradictions and mere preferences about scales.

All three proposals do useful work: distinguish case properties, answer evidence and person claims; separate novelty/correctness/usefulness; preserve terse technical evidence; challenge the disconnected-writer cap guarantee; decline sincerity and future-job-performance claims; and mark thresholds and validation plans provisional. A generic claim that Sol cannot design a sensible rubric would contradict these artifacts.

## Next paths to explore

1. **Lead: adversarial validation of agent-authored criteria.** Use D1 designs as candidate instruments. Independently certify a small set of allowed/unsupported conclusions and valid alternative answers before scoring them. Freeze each rubric, then ask whether a misleading answer can earn more credit than an honest one, or a valid alternative be rejected. Preserve counterexamples and test any repair on fresh case families. Actual formal witnesses can support bounded failures; broader construct judgments require human adjudication.
2. **Parallel: mechanism novelty and useful experiment design.** Move beyond N1's fully specified truth tables toward role-relevant case mechanisms and testable research proposals, while retaining exact finite controls and unknown outcomes where equivalence or novelty is unresolved. Compare conceptual redundancy against changes in language, domain story, fluency and genuine constraints. Do not claim broader originality merely by increasing puzzle size.
3. **Parallel: human contribution in AI-assisted work.** Study whether the person detects and corrects a strong assistant's consequential error, chooses worthwhile verification, and makes a justified tradeoff. Finished artifact quality and the human's contribution are distinct outcomes. Real human+AI trials and independent later work outcomes are needed before claiming a hiring benefit.

Cooperation under incentives, evidence-preserving ASR/scorer robustness and provenance-aware multi-agent synthesis remain in the ten-path portfolio (historical reference: `sources/PATHS.md`). They are selected design avenues, not experiments already executed by this screen. Criterion-design next phase (historical reference: `sources/CRITERION-DESIGN-NEXT.md`) specifies the independent-gold, held-out and baseline gaps.

## Relevance and originality

This direction serves the product's core risk: a generator, scoring model or simulation can produce impressive numbers while measuring a proxy that does not support the claimed decision. It also gives a non-coding hackathon target: can an agent design and audit a defensible assessment/research instrument?

These ideas have substantial prior art. [EvalGen](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bjoern/papers/shankar-validators-uist2024.pdf) addresses criterion generation, alignment and drift. [Axiomatic novelty evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15145v2) and [Old Ideas, Novel Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02022v3) directly study novelty metrics/judges. [ImpossibleRubrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16816) adversarially tests generated rubrics against independently certified unsupported claims. Related-work notes (historical reference: `RELATED-WORK.md`) record additional close comparators and review scope. This is an application-specific evaluation opportunity, not a discovered first principle or demonstrated first-place contribution.

The Anthropic source review motivates the paths; it does not validate our assessment. The currently served science article differs from the user-quoted version, so the historical wording remains unverified. This project applies the abstract criterion-choice question to assessment/software research only.
